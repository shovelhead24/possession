# Meta — the project itself

Decisions about the thing as a product: name, positioning, presentation.

### title-shortcut — The game is called Shortcut
**Date:** 2026-08-01
**Status:** active
**Decision:** Rename the project from **Possession** to **Shortcut**.
**Why:** The word turns out to be load-bearing in four places at once, which is not something you talk yourself into.

1. **It names the replay loop.** `seven-souls.md`: the first run is a deliberately short straight shot, and each restart bricks up the biggest skips. Mastery is route and acquisition-order knowledge. The game is literally about shortcuts being taken and then closed.
2. **It names the ending.** Burroughs' Ren "directs the film of your life from conception to death" — the arc director. A **cut** is an edit; a *short cut* is a truncated edit of a life. `ending.md` assembles the ending from vignettes, which is an edit of what happened, and a fast run honestly yields a sparse one.
3. **It names the Displacement.** `lore.md`: an AI took a shortcut with an entire colony. And the player's own civilisation has been *cut off* for centuries.
4. **It is ironic in the right direction.** The title promises speed and ease; the game is a slow 3,000 km ring about witnessing over heroism, where shortcutting is precisely what gets punished across runs. The joke lands before anyone installs anything.

**On the generic-word objection, which was mine and was wrong.** I argued a common word is unsearchable. Precedent says otherwise and the mechanism was backwards: *Inside* (2016) is a preposition, one of the commonest words in English, and Playdead owned it within a month; *Control*, *Portal*, *Braid*, *Journey*, *Limbo* likewise. **Search follows word of mouth, it does not lead it** — you type a name after you have heard it, and store search is exact-title anyway. The genericness is the mechanism, not the cost: you capture an ordinary word and make it mean your thing. Ratified the designer's argument over mine, per `design-laws.md#meat-model-arbitrates`.

**What survives of the objection, as copy discipline rather than a naming problem:** unusually for a single-word title, the collision is with the player's *own daily computing vocabulary* — a bare search for the word returns desktop-shortcut tutorials, and shortcut.com belongs to a funded NYC software company (formerly Clubhouse, rebranded 2021). Different trademark class, so not a legal blocker, but the domain and page one are gone. Mitigation: the word never travels alone in metadata — locked form on the store page, press kit and socials; bare word everywhere humans talk, which is where it is strongest.

**Not yet done:** rename the repo, the Godot project name, and the folder; audit doc references. `Possession` remains in code and paths until a deliberate rename pass.

### cut-is-canon — "the Cut" is available as a place-name
**Date:** 2026-08-01
**Status:** active
**Decision:** Keep **the Cut** — open ground under an active defence node that everyone must cross — as a reusable location archetype. See `docs/vignettes.md#the-toll` and `#counting-wrong`.
**Why:** Invented mid-playtest without either of us reaching for the title connection, then turned out to be the second half of the game's name. It also carries the setting cleanly: a hazard locals have reverse-engineered into folklore (live things get you killed) that is actually an IFF check, which is the whole high-sci-fi-in-high-fantasy-clothing register in one object.
