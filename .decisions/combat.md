# Combat

Planning surface (what exists, open questions) is `docs/combat.md`. This file holds the ratified rules.

### ammunition-is-the-balance-lever — Balance weapons on scarcity, not damage numbers
**Date:** 2026-08-17
**Status:** active
**Decision:** The lever that separates two weapons is what a kill **costs you in rounds** and how often the weapon runs you dry — not its per-hit damage. Two weapons with the same time-to-kill are not the same weapon if one empties in three kills and the other in thirty. Balance-tuning sessions read the ammo economy first (`--range` now prints `rd/kill` and `kill/mag` per weapon per range) and reach for the `damage` field last. Raising damage to make a weapon "better" is the move this rule forbids: it flattens the roster toward one shape (whoever has the biggest number wins), whereas moving `mag`, `reload_s` and the round's real supply keeps every weapon a distinct proposition about *how much fire you can bring before you can't*.
**Why:** The player arrives stripped and recovers capability under deprivation (`.decisions/progression.md#recovery-not-invention`); a world where you own your rifle but not a reliable belt of ammunition is exactly that theme at the scale of a single firefight, and it is free — the deprivation the story already asserts becomes the thing the combat math already turns on. It also satisfies `consumer-audit` (`.decisions/design-laws.md`): the scarcity quantity has two named player consumers — the shooter watching the magazine go dry (surfaced diegetically off the weapon per `diegetic-tools-not-hud`, never a free HUD counter) and, at design time, the balance table's ammo-economy columns. And it protects the tone guard ("mobile, not mighty"): a damage-number arms race is power fantasy; a supply problem is a survival one.
**Corollaries:**
- **`mag` and `reload_s` are first-class balance fields, not flavour.** They were defined on `weapon_def.gd` from the start and until now only fed the ttk math; they are now the primary dials, alongside the round's real scarcity.
- **Total carried rounds and resupply are deliberately NOT modelled yet.** Where ammunition *comes from* — found, salvaged, traded, taken — is the next queue item ("where weapons come from") and rides the same acquisition hook as vehicles (draws). Until that lands, `--range` measures the per-magazine economy; the campaign-scale supply economy is downstream of the acquisition work, not invented here.
- **The classes that have no magazine pay scarcity elsewhere.** A bow/launcher (`mag` 0) pays per shot and per draw/reload; energy pays in heat and charge; guided pays in `bulk`. `kill/mag` is left blank for them in the table rather than forced — scarcity is the lever for all of them, but its unit differs by class.
