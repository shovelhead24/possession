# Progression

Planning surface (tiers, arc, open questions) is `docs/progression.md`. This file holds the ratified rules.

### recovery-not-invention — The tech tree is climbed back UP, not discovered
**Date:** 2026-08-17
**Status:** active
**Decision:** Progression **recovers** capability the player already had; it never **invents** it from scratch. The player arrives from a spacefaring civilisation and is stripped of it at the roadside (`the-toll`, docs/vignettes.md), so a sharpened stick is not tier-one naïveté — it is improvisation under deprivation. "A spear is what you use when they took your rifle." The three tiers (Survival → Medieval → Ancient Alien, docs/progression.md) are therefore a climb back to a baseline the player has already seen, not an ascent into the unknown. This inverts the usual tech-tree shape: the player knows what the top looks like from the first minute, because they owned it.
**Why:** The inversion is free narrative leverage and it closes design questions without adding systems. The player keeps the *knowledge* of the top of the tree while losing the *means* — which is exactly what makes the-toll's central irony load-bearing rather than decorative ("something you could read off a panel in six seconds if they had left you anything to read it with"): the player **is** the panel the locals lack. Its original "mobile, not mighty" rationale is refined by `capability-reinterprets-place` below: recovery can feel powerful without granting control over history. Distinct from — but rhyming with — the *world's* recovery: settlements climbing back from the Displacement collapse are the `recovery_band`/`tech_level` field (`.decisions/civilization.md`, `.decisions/lore.md`); the player is the same story at the scale of one person, stripped by a folk toll instead of by a rogue AI.
**Corollaries:**
- **Gate = re-acquisition, never research.** Tiers advance by getting capability *back* — found, salvaged, traded, taken (the "where weapons/vehicles come from" queue items), plus faction trust and geography. There is no research/tech-point tree. Consistent with `no-missions` and "the world reshapes opportunity gradients, never rewards directly" (`.decisions/design-laws.md`).
- **No deep craft-from-scratch layer.** You do not build a rifle; you recover one. Crafting is bottom-tier improvisation only (sharpen a stick, bind a haft), kept shallow so it does not fight the witnessing tone. This resolves the inventory/crafting open question toward *acquired capabilities*, not a crafting economy.
- **The FP carbine is the test loadout, not the arc's opening.** The real arc opens stripped (Survival tier, no weapons); the immediate carbine is dev convenience, to be replaced when the stripped start lands.
- **Knowledge runs ahead of capability.** Unlike a normal tree where you learn a tier and gain it in the same step, here the player retains what is *possible* after losing the *means*. The knowledge ladder (knowledge.md) leads the capability ladder — and the transport ladder is the same shape's shadow (world.md, progression.md).
**Still open (not ratified here):** the exact tier gates, whether capability differs between the two protagonists in co-op, and death/save costs per tier. Those stay Open Questions in docs/progression.md.

### capability-reinterprets-place — Real power, continuous geography and attachment
**Date:** 2026-09-10
**Status:** active
**Decision:** Retain recovery-not-invention. Major capabilities should reinterpret familiar places and preserve earlier relationships and consequences across tiers. Mobility is real power; local mastery is compatible with witnessing events larger than the player. Replace the old "mobile, not mighty" constraint with power that expands choices without commanding history. No mandatory revisit checklist is implied.
**Why:** The progression arc earns meaning when a former obstacle becomes a route and a familiar place remains worth returning to. Three capability tiers must transform one experience rather than discard earlier play.
