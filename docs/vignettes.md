# Vignettes — a pool, not a bible

Area doc, started **2026-08-01**, out of a chat playtest of the text slice.

Each entry is self-contained and carries **only the context it actually spends**. They are not
connected to each other and are **not required to be consistent with each other** — two entries may
assume different fates for the colony, different theories of the AI, different tech levels. That is
deliberate. Consistency is a property of a *run*, assembled at play time; it is not a property of
the pool.

**Why the pool, not a bible.** Playtesting the text slice, the scene that worked was one that used
about four facts and invented the rest at the point of need. The deep background turned out to be
circumstantial — swap the faction, the machine, the era, and the scene still lands. This is
`.decisions/design-laws.md#no-authored-graphs` arriving from the fiction side: pools degrade into
repetition, trees degrade into dead ends, and we cannot afford trees.

**Standing constraint (locked).** The player **arrives** — from a spacefaring civilisation cut off
for centuries, possibly at war with itself or with something else. Not a native. Everything built so
far assumes arrival and it is not up for renegotiation. The register is **high science fiction in
high fantasy clothing**: locals live in omens, shrines and tribes; the substrate is transponders,
air defence and power cells; the tech tree is the bridge the player climbs between the two.

Each entry declares:
- **Needs** — the minimum that must be true for it to play.
- **Generalises** — what can be swapped without breaking it.

## Arrival

### falling-long — The descent is boring
**Needs:** the ring is enormous; the player is coming down.
**Generalises:** any vehicle, any era, any reason for the fall.

The worst part is how long it takes. Terminal, stable, nothing to do. The ring does not grow the way
a planet grows — it resolves, laterally, in both directions at once, until the horizon is a straight
line above you as well as below and you understand that you have been reading the sky wrong for
several minutes. There is time to eat. Nobody has ever mentioned that there is time to eat.

### first-word — Someone finds you before you find anyone
**Needs:** the player came down; a local; no shared language yet.
**Generalises:** any biome, any faction, hostile or not.

She does not help you up. She walks a slow circle at four metres, twice, looking at the ground more
than at you — at the marks the landing made, the burn line, the way the scrub is laid flat — and
whatever she works out from that, she works out before she has looked at your face once. Then she
says a short sentence, twice, with a gap in the middle where your answer is supposed to go.

### wrong-air — It is breathable and that is worse
**Needs:** arrival; a suit or a seal.
**Generalises:** any environmental system on the ring working correctly.

The readout says the air is fine. Not survivable-fine — *maintained*. Oxygen fraction inside a
tolerance no accident holds for nine hundred years. You take the seal off because there is no reason
not to, and it smells of cut grass and hot dust and, faintly, underneath, of the inside of a machine.
Something is still running this. It has been running it the whole time nobody was watching.

## Locals, and being read by them

### the-toll — They take your civilisation at the roadside
**Needs:** a hazard locals have empirically mapped; the player's kit is the thing that triggers it.
**Generalises:** the hazard can be anything — a defence node, a scanner, a hunting animal, weather.

The fee is not money. They go through your kit with you standing there and take everything that
holds a charge, naming things you had stopped thinking of as objects, and they are neither cruel nor
apologetic about it because to them this is simply the price of the ground. They are also right.
What they cannot tell you, because they have never had the words for it, is *why* — they have a
rule that works and no theory under it, and the rule is a folk translation of something you could
read off a panel in six seconds if they had left you anything to read it with.

### counting-wrong — The board only counts departures
**Needs:** a dangerous passage; someone selling access to it.
**Generalises:** any one-way trip, any broker.

The number goes up on the board every evening and it is a count of people who set off. Nobody has
ever come back to say otherwise, because coming back is not a thing anyone who makes it has a reason
to do. The trade rests entirely on an absence of complaints from people who are structurally
incapable of complaining, and the man who owns the board is not lying to you. He has simply never had
occasion to notice.

### bad-guess — They are wrong about you in a specific way
**Needs:** the player is visibly not local; a faction with a cosmology.
**Generalises:** the wrong guess can be flattering, damning, or merely inconvenient.

They have decided what you are. Not vaguely — precisely, with a name for it and a set of things that
are now expected, and a couple of people who are already unhappy about it. Correcting them is
available and costs more than you think, because the story they have put you in was load-bearing for
somebody before you turned up, and that somebody is in the room.

### the-ask — Nobody assigns you anything
**Needs:** a settlement under pressure; the player passing through.
**Generalises:** any pressure, any settlement.

He tells you the whole thing. Where the water went, whose fault, what they tried, what it will mean
by the dry end of the year. It takes ten minutes and it is not a request. At no point does he ask
you for anything, and when he is finished he thanks you for listening and goes back to work, and the
problem is still sitting there afterwards, entirely available and entirely unclaimed.

## The ring as an object

### the-wall — Scale refuses to resolve
**Needs:** the wall; daylight.
**Generalises:** any ring-scale structure.

It does not look far away, it looks *wrong*. The eye keeps trying to put it at a normal distance and
the haze keeps refusing, so it reads as a painted backdrop that someone has hung too close, until a
bird crosses in front of it and takes eleven seconds to clear something you had filed as a doorway.

### night-lights — Somebody's grandmother is down there
**Needs:** night; the far side of the ring visible overhead.
**Generalises:** any populated arc.

The far side comes up after dark as a band of scattered light directly overhead, and you can pick out
the shape of settlement in it — clusters, a line where a road must be, a dark patch that is probably
water. It is four hundred kilometres straight up and every one of those lights is a room. You will
never go there. Nobody you meet has ever been there. It is over your head every single night.

### still-running — A machine that does not care
**Needs:** any piece of working ring infrastructure.
**Generalises:** pumps, lifts, lights, defence, drainage.

It does the thing. It has been doing the thing since before the language you are hearing existed, it
will do the thing after, and it has no interface, no status light and no opinion. People have built a
shrine at the base of it, which it also has no opinion about. The shrine is younger than the last
service interval.

## Traces

### provenance — A body with a history
**Needs:** a corpse or a ruin; the player able to read manufacture.
**Generalises:** any object with an origin the locals cannot see.

The locals think it is old. It is not old, it is *foreign* — the weave is machine-laid, the seams are
ultrasonic, and it came off a production line that could not have existed on this ring at any point in
its history. Which means either it came down like you did, or something here can still make things
like this, and you cannot currently tell which of those is worse.

### the-second-site — A draw with nothing at the end of it
**Needs:** a distant visible anomaly; the player free to ignore it.
**Generalises:** any far object; wreck, tower, fire, structure.

You can see it from the ridge and you can see it from the valley two days later, which is the whole
of its power. Nobody has told you to go. Nobody has told you not to. Everyone local has an opinion
about it and none of them have been, and the reasons they give for not going get less convincing the
more of them you collect.

## The thing that moved everybody here

### the-displacement — Two people, two stories
**Needs:** the colony was relocated; the reason is disputed.
**Generalises:** the AI may be present, gone, dead, or listening.

He says they were brought here to be saved. She says the same events, in the same order, with the
same dates, and it is a story about being *taken*. Neither of them is arguing; they have both known
the other's version their whole lives and long ago stopped finding it interesting. You are the first
person in either of their lifetimes for whom the difference is news.

### it-answers — Something responds and should not have
**Needs:** the player has working comms or a live system.
**Generalises:** any channel, any era.

You were not calling anything. You were running a diagnostic, out loud, from habit, and something on
a band nobody here uses acknowledged — not words, an acknowledgement, the protocol handshake your
own people use to say *received*. It does not repeat. It does not do it again for eleven days.

## Repetition, and being known

### seen-you-before — The world remembers a run you do not
**Needs:** a second or later playthrough; cross-run memory.
**Generalises:** any region the player worked in previously.

The gate is bricked. Not barricaded — bricked, properly, with mortar and time, by people who had a
specific reason to close a specific gap. Nobody mentions it. Nobody knows why you are standing there
looking at it. There is no version of asking that does not sound insane.

### the-dossier — Being liked by the wrong thing
**Needs:** the AI has been watching across runs.
**Generalises:** any observer with continuity.

It has your habits. Not your name — your *habits*: the order you do things in, the approach you
prefer, the thing you always check first. The evidence for this is not a speech, it is that three
separate situations this week have been shaped exactly wrong for you in a way that random would not
manage, and once you notice you cannot stop noticing.

## Leaving

### the-axis — Up is a direction again
**Needs:** an axis structure; the ending available.
**Generalises:** any route off the ring.

Gravity thins as you climb and the ring closes overhead into a tube and then a tunnel, and somewhere
in there the floor stops being the floor. It is the first genuinely vertical thing you have seen since
you came down. Everything on this world has been *along*.

### sparse-ending — An honest short run
**Needs:** the run is ending; few facts accumulated.
**Generalises:** any ending, any length of run.

There is not much to say about it and it does not pretend otherwise. Three things happened, and they
are named, and two of them you were only near. Nobody tells you what else there was. The ending is
the size the run was, which is its own kind of respect.

## Open

- How long is a vignette allowed to be? These run 60–130 words. The playtest note was **shorter,
  reveal less, hold detail in reserve** — see below.
- **Detail ageing.** A held-back detail should decay if it is not spent: keep a small per-scene
  reserve, and let unspent entries expire rather than accumulate into a backlog that gets
  shoehorned in later. Not yet specified. Belongs with the bake/realization work.
- Do vignettes carry **tags** for precondition matching (biome, faction posture, run index, player
  knowledge tier), or are they hand-picked by the director? Tags, presumably, per `no-authored-graphs`.
- Several of these are the same shape as `docs/moments/` director's notes. Decide whether moments
  become a subset of this pool or stay separate.
